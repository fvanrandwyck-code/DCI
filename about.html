About Digital Connectivity Insights

Digital Connectivity Insights is a monthly analysis of the relationship between government and the telecoms industry — why policymakers are making the decisions they're making about digital infrastructure, and what that means for the people building, running, and investing in it.

What this is

Telecoms trade press covers what happened. General policy and geopolitics commentary covers government decisions in the abstract. Neither usually explains the two together: what government actually wants from digital infrastructure, why, and how that's likely to play out for the industry building it.

Digital Connectivity Insights sits in that gap. Each issue takes one significant policy question — a merger under review, a spectrum decision, a piece of legislation, a divergence opening up between UK and EU rules — and asks three things: what is government actually trying to achieve here, what's the political and institutional logic behind it, and what does that imply is coming next.

This is analysis, not news. If you want to know what happened this week, the trade press already does that well. If you want to understand where things are heading and why, that's what this is for.

Who it's for

Primarily: people in the telecoms industry — operators, vendors, investors, and other public affairs professionals — who need to anticipate government's direction of travel rather than just react to it once it's public.

Secondarily: people in and around government and policy — civil servants, political staff, think tanks, journalists — who understand policy machinery well but may not have deep day-to-day fluency in how the telecoms industry actually works, what its commercial pressures are, or why a given technical detail matters more than it looks.

Each issue is written to be useful to both — enough industry context that a policy reader isn't lost, enough political and institutional context that an industry reader understands why government is behaving the way it is, not just what it's doing.

Who's writing it

Digital Connectivity Insights is written in a personal capacity by someone whose background spans telecoms and digital public affairs — including time inside UK government at DSIT, public affairs work with EU institutions in Brussels, and a current role in telecoms public affairs at techUK. That gives the author a working understanding of both how these policy decisions get made and how the industry actually experiences them.

Independence and disclosure

This publication is written and published independently, in a personal capacity, and does not represent the views of any employer or organisation.

A few ground rules:

  • No writing about specific transactions, clients, or live lobbying positions the author is personally or professionally involved in.
  • Where a relevant professional connection to a topic exists, it will be disclosed explicitly rather than left implicit.
  • The goal is analysis that's useful regardless of whose "side" it lands on — including when that means saying things the industry, or government, won't love hearing.

If you think that standard hasn't been met, say so.

How often, and what to expect

Digital Connectivity Insights publishes monthly. Each issue is a single deep dive rather than a roundup — expect it to take you longer than five minutes to read, and to leave you with a clearer view of where a specific policy question is heading, not just a summary of where it stands today.

The Tracker

Alongside the monthly analysis, the site hosts a Tracker — a regularly-updated feed of UK digital infrastructure policy developments from DCMS, Ofcom, and DBIST, filtered for telecoms relevance. It's curated, not analysed: headlines and brief context, designed to surface what's moving.
